Project 0: Becoming Friends with Your Camera

By: Stephane Finot

Overview

In this project, I explore the relationship between different perspectives and zooms to see the effect of different focal lengths.

Part 1: Selfie: The Wrong Way vs. The Right Way

Images taken from a further distance look more better because facial features are more proportional. When we view human faces, typically view them from a distance. For very distinct features, our eyes, nose, and mouths make up a central portion of our face. However, when you look from too close like with the wide angle lenses, the features begin to take over a majority of the sillouhette. We can also imagine when we look from too close, we are no longer able to see the ears of the subject, making a perspective that lacks fundamental features of a human.

Part 2: Architectural Perspective Compression

The first picture taken far away looks flatter because the relative distance between the camera and is greater, causing the distance in the pillars to be relatively smaller than in the second picture. In the second picture, the camera is closer to the pillars, causing the distance between the pillars to be relatively larger and have more depth.
